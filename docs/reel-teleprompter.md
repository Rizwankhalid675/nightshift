# Reel teleprompter — read this, nothing else

Twenty-three lines over **2:33** of narration, inside a 2:42 film. About 376 words,
so roughly **147 a minute** — slower than conversation. If you reach the end of a line early, stop
and wait for the next caption. Do not improvise and do not speed up.

**The captions stay on screen under your voice.** They are the accessible track and they carry the
film when someone watches muted. You are not reading them aloud — each line below says a little
more than the caption it sits under.

**A 3-second title card runs first.** Do not speak over it. Your first word lands when the first
caption appears, and `build-reel.sh` offsets your take by that automatically. An end card holds
after your last word.

**Timecodes are the caption cuts**, generated by `./scripts/build-reel.sh --timeline`.
`tests/video.test.js` fails if this file and the build ever disagree.

## Recording

1. Play `video/nightshift_reel.mp4` once without speaking, to learn the caption cuts.
2. Record with QuickTime Player (**File → New Audio Recording**) or Voice Memos. Quiet room, mic
   close in, **one continuous take** — do not stop between lines.
3. Start recording, start the video, and say your first word when the **first caption** appears at
   0:03 — not over the title card.
4. Lay the take over the reel:

   ```bash
   ./scripts/build-reel.sh ~/path/to/your-recording.m4a
   ```

   It reports the drift against the 154-second narration window and writes
   `video/nightshift_reel_narrated.mp4`.

**Do not mux it by hand with `-shortest`.** A take shorter than the picture makes `-shortest` end
the file at the end of the *audio* — a 30-second take against the other cut produced a 37-second
video, with the live AWS footage gone and exit status 0. The script pads instead.

If a line runs long, let it. The caption will change on time whatever you do, and a calm voice
slightly behind the cut reads better than a rushed one chasing it.
